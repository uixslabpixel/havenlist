SF Bay Area Housing Market 2026

The San Francisco Bay Area housing market kicked off 2026 with a significant change in its dynamics. Following two years characterized by limited inventory and swift price increases, we are now observing initial indications of a rebalancing trend. The San Francisco Bay Area housing market began 2026 with a notable shift in its dynamics. After two years marked by low inventory and rapid price hikes, we're now seeing early signs of a rebalancing trend. However, it's crucial to recognize that certain key neighborhoods continue to show strength and resilience in this changing environment.

Inventory is rising

In the first quarter of 2026, the Bay Area saw a significant 8% increase in active listings compared to the same period last year. Notably, the neighborhoods of Pacific Heights and Noe Valley welcomed the highest number of new listings, while Marina and Sausalito experienced tighter inventory levels. For prospective buyers, this change means a wider selection of homes and a slight reduction in competition during open house events.

Prices holding steady

Despite the rise in inventory, the median sale price in San Francisco held steady at $1.35 million, showing minimal change from the fourth quarter of 2025. The luxury market, especially homes priced above $2 million, saw a modest increase of 3%, driven by sustained interest from tech executives and international investors.

What to watch in Q2

Three signals will shape the spring market: the direction of mortgage rates, the pace of new listings in Pacific Heights and Noe Valley, and how quickly well-priced homes go under contract.

Buyers should keep pre-approvals current and be ready to move when a good home appears, while sellers can benefit from pricing strategically and preparing their home early.
